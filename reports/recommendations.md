# Recommendations
### India Packaged Food Label Impact & Market Analytics

These recommendations follow directly from the findings. They are **decision
support**, not financial advice, and they deliberately avoid inventing rupee
figures — impact is expressed in the exposure and priority indices the analysis
actually produced.

---

## Part A — Stakeholder playbook

### 1. Manufacturers (FMCG brand owners)
- **Problem:** ~48% of the market is D/E, and exposure is concentrated in
  commercially important indulgence categories (confectionery, biscuits, salty
  snacks) where a brand's whole range can be Red.
- **Impact:** under the Moderate scenario, ~6,100 D/E SKUs of demand are exposed
  to switching; the highest Revenue-Exposure and Reformulation-Priority scores
  all sit in these categories.
- **Decision:** treat the label as a reformulation roadmap, not a threat.
- **Recommendation:** prioritise the **fixable grade-D lines** (those just below
  the C boundary on sugar/energy/sodium) in high-commercial-importance
  categories; protect and *grow* existing A/B ranges as a marketing asset;
  pre-empt the beverage split by accelerating reduced-sugar variants (the
  full-sugar subset is 85% D/E while diet/zero variants already reach grade B).

### 2. Retailers (modern trade / e-grocery such as BigBasket)
- **Problem:** a colour label reshapes shopper attention across the entire
  assortment simultaneously; own-label and category ranges are equally exposed.
- **Impact:** category mix, planogram and promotion strategy all move; premium
  price tiers offer no nutritional cover (budget categories score *best*).
- **Decision:** use the label as a range-curation and private-label opportunity.
- **Recommendation:** build "Green/Amber/Red" navigation and filters; expand
  A/B private-label in exposed categories; avoid leaning on "premium =
  healthier" messaging, which the price-vs-nutrition analysis contradicts;
  use category exposure to guide healthier-checkout and end-cap decisions.

### 3. Consumers
- **Problem:** nutritional quality is currently hard to see on-pack, and price is
  a poor proxy for it.
- **Impact:** shoppers systematically over-estimate the healthiness of pricier
  products.
- **Decision:** shop by grade, not by price.
- **Recommendation:** an A–E colour label materially improves the ability to
  compare within a category (the evidence base shows interpretive labels change
  choice at the margin); category-level guidance (staples/dairy/produce skew
  Green; confectionery/biscuits/oils skew Red) is a useful heuristic.

### 4. Policymakers (FSSAI)
- **Problem:** designing an effective front-of-pack scheme (the star-based INR)
  and anticipating industry response.
- **Impact:** exposure is concentrated, so a label's effect will be felt
  category-by-category; reformulation incentives matter as much as consumer
  nudges (meta-analytic evidence shows labels drive supply-side reformulation).
- **Decision:** design for both channels — consumer clarity *and* reformulation
  incentive.
- **Recommendation:** an interpretive, graded scheme with clear thresholds
  creates the strongest reformulation pull; monitor the categories flagged
  highest-risk here; note that the exact grade distribution under INR (stars)
  will differ from Nutri-Score and should be validated on India-specific
  composition data as it becomes available.

## Part B — Strategic options for a manufacturer

Four archetypal responses, scored qualitatively (High/Medium/Low). No monetary
values are invented; "benefit" is framed as exposure reduction and brand equity.

| Option | Cost | Benefit | Risk | Feasibility | When it fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Reformulate** (cut sugar/sodium/energy on fixable D lines) | Medium–High (R&D, taste, supply) | **High** — moves D→C/B, cuts exposure at source, earns a better on-pack grade | Medium (taste/acceptance) | **Medium–High** for grade-D lines near the boundary | Core indulgence ranges with many "fixable" D products |
| **2. Reposition** (portion, occasion, "treat" framing, premiumise) | Low–Medium | Medium — protects margin without changing recipe | Medium — label still Red; limited consumer cover | High | Categories where reformulation hurts the product essence (premium chocolate) |
| **3. Portfolio shift** (grow A/B ranges, acquire/launch healthier lines) | High | **High, durable** — structurally lowers portfolio exposure and captures Green demand | Medium (execution, time) | Medium | Brands over-indexed in Red categories seeking long-term hedge |
| **4. Do nothing** | None now | None | **High** — full exposure to demand switching and reputational risk under a mandatory label | High (trivially) | Only defensible for already-Green portfolios (dairy, produce, staples) |

**Recommended default:** a blend of **Option 1 (reformulate the fixable D lines)**
and **Option 3 (grow the A/B range)**, sequenced by the Reformulation-Priority
ranking — Chocolate & Confectionery first, then salty snacks, spreads and
sauces. "Do nothing" is only rational for portfolios already concentrated in the
Green staples/dairy/produce categories.
